##### WORK LOG #####

The aim of this project is to identify a notable period in the company's sales data and investigate what caused the observed change.

#### DATASET ####

Initially loaded the AdventureWorks dataset into SQL Server Management Studio to unpack it. Then, downloaded any relevant tables as .csv files to transfer them to GCP Cloud Storage. Next, created a dataset in BigQuery to begin SQL analysis. (Connected the GitHub repo to GCP.)

#### WHAT TO INVESTIGATE? ####

The first query I want to write is intended to get an overview of how revenue, orders, customers, and units changed from month to month. This will help in checking for any notable changes that occurred throughout the company's history, which can provide a starting point for the intended research.

The results for the first query show several periods that could be analysed. The first, second, and last rows are immediately discarded, since they contain initial or incomplete company data. Although there are several periods with large fluctuations in monthly revenue, in most cases, the customers, orders, and units fluctuate synchronously with revenue, suggesting that it can be explained entirely through sales volume.

Other periods are more interesting because the change in revenue cannot be explained simply by changes in orders, customers, and units. In particular, between Jan and Feb 2013, revenue decreases by 10%, while the customers, orders, and units all increase by over 100%. This makes the period between January and February 2013 particularly interesting for further investigation.

#### INVESTIGATION ####

Why did revenue decrease between Jan and Feb 2013 despite the massive increase in orders, customers, and units?

To answer this question, I want to look at the following ideas:
  - Main metrics: 
    - total revenue/orders/customers/units 
    - revenue per order/unit  
    - units per order 
  
  - Products: 
    - revenue/orders/units by product category/subcategory 
    - change in selling price by product category/subcategory 

  - Promotions: 
    - check for promotions 
    - number of promoted sales 
    - average discount  
    - revenue from promoted vs non-promoted sales 
    - discount by product  
  
  - Customers: 
    - new vs returning customers  
    - revenue/orders/units per customer  
    - average order value per customer 
    - potentially customer demographics  
  
  - Locations: 
    - revenue/orders/customers/units by territory 
    - revenue/orders/customers by country/state 
  
  - Other: 
    - potentially change in sales reason 
    - previous Jan to Feb main metrics

As the final project goal is to create a Power BI dashboard, I will use SQL to get the main data for analysis before moving to Power BI (utilising data modelling and DAX) to carry out the remaining analysis. 

Initial look at the products table:  
- mostly positive across the board, with more sales across most items  
- current explanations for the revenue drop:  
  - a decrease in sale price of a key revenue driver 
  - far fewer sales of the subcategory with the highest revenue share 
  - three subcategories make up 96.76% of revenue for Jan, 91.24% in Feb (weakness in the business model) 
- potential for a hierarchical chart with category -> subcategory
 
New idea to add to the products query. Standalone, revenue is not a good indicator of performance, so I will add revenue share, which is more informative since it describes the percentage of revenue from that product. With the revenue share for both months, the tree graph can show revenue as square size and percentage-point change as colour.  

Initial look at the promotions table:  
- no obvious change in promotions or discounts that could explain the revenue decline 
- far more orders for accessories and clothing in Feb compared to Jan, and fewer bike orders, which is a huge revenue hit  
  - For example, for the sales with no discount:
    - 340 clothing units sold account for ~12,000  
    - 20 bikes sold account for ~67,000  
 
Since the promotions do not provide an explanation for the revenue decline, I will not use the promotion by categories table. The table is too complex and conveys the same information as the products table. Instead, I will create a new query to summarise the promotional metrics across Jan and Feb.
